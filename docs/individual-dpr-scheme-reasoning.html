AI DPR · Product rationale

Why Individual Latest DPR is scheme-aware, and why each scheme asks different questions

Internal briefing · August 2025 · Maps product behaviour in Create New Latest DPR to published scheme / bank practice. Not a legal opinion or a substitute for current MoMSME / bank circulars.

One-line answer. Cluster DPR is written for a group project (SPV, CFC, member units). An individual entrepreneur applying for a specific scheme should not fill that paperwork. Each scheme also has a different decision to make (toolkit vs street-vendor loan vs term loan vs food-processing grant vs state industrial incentive). We only surface questions that feed that decision, and we hide bank-style viability tables when the scheme never uses them.

1. Why these product changes exist

Two users, two documents

The existing Cluster DPR flow is the right shape for MSE-CDP-style work: many units, a Special Purpose Vehicle, a Common Facility Centre, shareholding, and cluster employment grids. Forcing a sole proprietor or street vendor through that form produces empty SPV sections, invented member counts, and a document that a scheme officer will not recognise.

Create New Latest DPR is a parallel builder for one unit. Cluster DPR is left unchanged. Preview, save, generate, and AI suggestions still run on the existing cluster APIs so we do not fork a second 5,000-line document renderer. Individual answers are mapped into the same section slots, tagged isIndividualDPR.

Scheme is the form controller

Venture Match already tells the entrepreneur which schemes they appear eligible for. Sending every match into a generic 18-step bank DPR wastes time and collects the wrong proofs. matchedSchemeCode (from the match button, the URL, or the on-page dropdown) is the single switch for extra questions, hidden steps, and uploads. Empty scheme means a vanilla bank term-loan DPR: simplified financials, no scheme extras.

Steps 1–14 stay a common “story of this unit” (who, where, what, cost, sales). That story is useful to almost every lender. Steps 15–18 and a few extras on step 1 / 12 / 18 change because the instrument is different, not because we enjoy extra fields.

Design rules we used

  • Ask for a decision, not a biography. If an officer or bank circular does not use a field, we do not show it.
  • Do not fake cluster machinery. No SPV board, CFC, member units, or “number of micro/small/medium enterprises” for one firm.
  • Hide what the scheme never underwrites. A ₹15,000 tool voucher does not need IRR, NPV, or a five-year DSCR table.
  • Proofs match the gazette / SOP, not our imagination. CoV for SVANidhi, FSSAI for food processing, FCI + CFE/CFO for AP EDP, and so on.
  • Reuse bank language where the product is a term loan. Cost vs means of finance, BEP, DSCR if there is a loan, CoD, jobs, turnover.

2. Shared individual questions (all schemes)

These stay on the form because any reader—bank, DIC, or SNA—still needs to know the unit. Labels are reworded for one entrepreneur.

StepsWhat we askWhy it stays
1–4 Unit name, district, location, nature of business, products, sector, unit profile Identity of the project. Cluster grids (enterprise count, age of enterprises, employment per unit) are dropped: there is only one unit.
5–9 Value chain, market, gaps, SWOT, planned interventions Narrative a credit officer or scheme file still uses to judge whether the activity is real and the gap is genuine.
10 Workplace / shed / unit (not CFC) Where production or vending happens. CFC language belongs to cluster infrastructure, not a shop or artisan workplace.
11 Applicant / firm: name, legal status, year, owners Who is contractually liable. SPV, board, shareholding pattern, and member-unit lists are cluster legal structure.
12–14 Project cost, means of finance, operating cost and sales Even small loans need a cost and a repayment story. Heavy land/building tables are hidden only when the scheme is explicitly micro (Mudra Shishu/Kishore).

Vanilla bank term loan (no scheme selected) — steps 15–18

This is the default individual pack when the entrepreneur wants a normal term loan without wrapping a named scheme.

  • 15 Viability. Cost vs equity + loan; five-year sales, RM, wages, power, net profit; break-even % of capacity. DSCR only if bank loan > 0, and we flag if average DSCR < 1.5 (common internal hurdle). No NPV/IRR/sensitivity — those belong to large project finance, not a micro/small unit file.
  • 16 Schedule. Commercial production date plus a few milestones (machinery, power, trial run). Banks care that the asset will exist before disbursement is fully drawn.
  • 17 Impact. Direct jobs and expected annual turnover — enough for priority-sector / MSME reporting without cluster-wide impact essays.
  • 18 Proofs. Aadhaar/PAN, Udyam, passbook, machinery quotations. Identity, MSME status, bank account, and that the capex is priced.

3. Scheme-specific questions and why

PM Vishwakarma

The live benefit in the product is a toolkit / voucher path for traditional artisans (about ₹15,000 of tools), plus identity checks. It is not a factory term loan. Five-year P&L, implementation Gantt, and “expected cluster impact” do not help a Vishwakarma file and they intimidate the applicant.

What we show or hideReason
Hide steps 15, 16, 17No bank DSCR exercise; no commercial-production schedule for a toolkit; employment/turnover tables are not the appraisal core.
Craft / trade (listed trades)Eligibility is trade-based. The officer must record which of the notified crafts the applicant practises.
Current tools vs new toolsThe voucher is for a defined toolkit upgrade. “What you use today” vs “what you will buy” is the actual procurement justification.
Uploads: Aadhaar, savings passbook, ration cardIdentity, bank account for DBT / credit, and household/family verification as used in enrolment practice. Not SPV deeds or member Udyam copies.

PM SVANidhi

This is a working-capital product for identified street vendors, typically in urban local-body areas, with a digital-payments push (UPI). Appraisal is “is this person a vendor we can onboard?”, not “does this CFC have a 1.5 DSCR?”

What we show or hideReason
Hide steps 15, 16, 17Ticket size and product type do not require a five-year project-finance pack.
Certificate of Vending vs Letter of RecommendationScheme SOPs accept CoV or, where CoV is not issued, a LoR from the ULB / Town Vending Committee. That is the eligibility proof, not a DPR.
UPI QR / UPI IDDigital payment adoption is an explicit scheme objective and often a condition for later tranches / incentives.
Upload: CoV or LoRWithout that document the application is not a SVANidhi file.

PMMY (Mudra) — Shishu / Kishore (budget under ₹5 lakh in our bands)

Mudra at this size is meant to be a simple shop / service / tiny manufacturing loan. RBI / IBA working-capital practice for such units (Nayak Committee lineage, widely used in MSME WC) estimates WC as a share of turnover with a small promoter margin, rather than a full land–building–P&M schedule.

What we show or hideReason
Hide heavy capex tables on step 12 (land, building, utilities, pre-operative)Shishu/Kishore files are rarely land-and-shed projects. Asking for FCI-style blocks invites made-up numbers.
Keep machinery + working-capital marginIf they are buying a machine or stocking inventory, those two lines are the real cost.
Projected annual turnover + Nayak WC (20% of turnover, 5% margin on that WC)Gives a transparent WC limit the branch can recognise. 20% / 5% is the product’s operational shorthand, labelled as such in the form.
Uploads: shop address proof, bank statements, equipment quotesExistence of the activity, banking habit / cashflow, and price of any asset financed.

Mudra Tarun Plus (higher bands, e.g. ₹50 lakh–₹1 crore in our handoff)

Larger Mudra is closer to a normal MSME term/WC loan. We keep the full individual 15–17 pack. We add previous Mudra closure / repayment certificate because Tarun Plus-type enhancements are typically for borrowers who have already run a Mudra account cleanly. Without closure proof the higher slab is not the right product.

PMEGP

PMEGP is a margin-money subsidy on a bankable project (KVIC / KVIB / DIC + bank). The bank still needs a short DPR: cost, means of finance, viability, and implementation. That is why we keep steps 15–16. Extra questions are only the statutory gates that stop a file at the counter.

What we show or hideReason
Keep 15–16 (and 17)The financing bank appraises a project, not a toolkit. BEP and a production date belong here.
Quotations + building estimateCapex must be supported; inflated machinery is a classic rejection reason.
Caste certificate if Venture Match owner is SC/ST/BCHigher subsidy rates for special categories. No certificate means the higher rate cannot be claimed.
8th-pass / education certificate if manufacturing cost > ₹10 lakh or service > ₹5 lakhPMEGP guidelines tie a minimum education standard to higher project cost. Below those cost ceilings we do not nag for a certificate.

PMFME (micro food processing)

PMFME subsidises formalisation of food units. Food Safety and Standards Authority of India registration (or a credible plan to obtain it) is not optional: you cannot legally run the unit the grant is paying for without a FSSAI path. Premises and machinery are the two big cost heads the SNA / bank will check.

What we show or hideReason
Keep 15–16It is still a capital subsidy on a project, so a short viability and commissioning story is needed.
FSSAI: already have vs will obtainSeparates units that are already formal from those whose “formalisation” is the point of the scheme.
Uploads: machinery quotes, premises lease, draft FSSAIAsset, place of business, and food-licence trail. Lease matters because many micro units do not own the kitchen/shed.

AP EDP (state industrial incentive / EDP path)

Andhra Pradesh industrial incentives are typically claimed against Fixed Capital Investment (land, building, plant & machinery), often with extra land-related benefits inside APIIC parks, and they require pollution and CA certification. Hiding land/building would break the claim.

What we show or hideReason
Keep FCI lines (land, building, P&M) on step 12Incentive is a % of eligible FCI. If we hide land/building, the claim cannot be computed.
APIIC park? yes/noPark location can change land cost / rebate treatment. It is a hint for the file, not a full GIS survey.
Uploads: land/shed allotment, CFE/CFO (single-desk pollution), CA-certified FCI statementTitle/allotment, environmental clearance, and a CA number the industries department will actually accept.

Other Venture Match schemes in v1 (Stand-Up India, CGTMSE, OBMMS, AP parks, RAMP, etc.)

Eligibility for those products is already captured in Venture Match (owner category, ticket size, Udyam, greenfield, and so on). The Latest DPR does not yet add a unique question pack for each of them. The entrepreneur still gets the individual 18-step bank-style DPR plus default identity/Udyam/quote uploads. That is deliberate v1 scope: we specialised the schemes where the document and proofs are structurally different (toolkit, vendor ID, food licence, FCI incentive), not where the difference is only “who is eligible”.

4. How this should feel when you test it

Changing the scheme dropdown will not rewrite steps 2–11. Those are the unit story. You should see: (1) a scheme banner stating what changed; (2) extra fields on step 1 for Vishwakarma, SVANidhi, PMFME, AP EDP; (3) fewer step pills when 15–17 are hidden; (4) a different upload list on step 18; (5) Mudra micro capex/Nayak only if Venture Match handed off a sub-₹5 lakh budget.

5. What we explicitly did not do

  • Did not alter Cluster DPR questions or layout.
  • Did not ship a separate PDF template per scheme in v1 — same generator, fewer empty sections.
  • Did not add Telugu for every new field in v1.
  • Did not treat this mapping as a replacement for the latest KVIC / MoHUA / MoFPI / GoAP circular. If a guideline changes, schemeFormConfig.ts is the place to update show/hide and uploads.
